A Ship That
Changed the War

The SS Jeremiah O'Brien is the last unaltered Liberty Ship in existence — a floating testament to American industrial might and the courage of the men and women who sailed her into history. Built in just 56 days at the New England Shipbuilding Corporation in South Portland, Maine, she was launched on June 19, 1943.

In her wartime service, the O'Brien completed seven voyages across hostile waters, carrying vital cargo to the Allied forces. Most remarkably, she made eleven Channel crossings during the D-Day operations at Normandy — the largest seaborne invasion in history.

In 1994, the O'Brien made an extraordinary return voyage to the beaches of Normandy for the 50th anniversary of D-Day, where she was reviewed by Her Majesty Queen Elizabeth II — the only ship to make that journey back. Since 1979, she has been maintained entirely by a dedicated volunteer crew, certified by the United States Coast Guard, and welcomes visitors at her permanent berth at Pier 35 in San Francisco.

1943 Built in 56 days at South Portland, Maine. Launched June 19 and immediately pressed into wartime service.
1944 Completed eleven Channel crossings during the Normandy invasion — Operation Overlord, the turning point of WWII.
1979 Restored by volunteers and permanently berthed in San Francisco as a living museum and National Historic Landmark.
1994 Made the historic return voyage to Normandy for the 50th anniversary, reviewed by Queen Elizabeth II — the only ship to do so.
Today The sole surviving unaltered Liberty Ship, U.S. Coast Guard certified, operated by 100% volunteer crew at Pier 35, San Francisco.
