Ship's History

The Last of Her Kind.
Still Sailing.

The SS Jeremiah O'Brien is the last unaltered Liberty Ship in existence. Of the 2,751 Liberty Ships built during World War II — the largest single shipbuilding program in history — she alone survives intact, unchanged from the day she launched.

"Built in 56 days. Survived the war. Returned to Normandy 50 years later. Still certified to sail by the U.S. Coast Guard."

Built in June 1943 at the New England Shipbuilding Corporation in South Portland, Maine, the Jeremiah O'Brien completed seven wartime voyages carrying vital cargo across the Atlantic. Her most celebrated achievement was serving in the D-Day invasion of Normandy — crossing the English Channel eleven times during the June 1944 landings, shuttling men and materiel to the beaches of France.

After the war she lay in the Suisun Bay Reserve Fleet, a ghost among ghosts — until 1979, when a dedicated crew of volunteers rescued her from the mothball fleet. Restored by hand, powered by original machinery, she became the flagship of the National Liberty Ship Memorial.

In 1994, on the 50th anniversary of D-Day, she made the voyage back to Normandy under her own power — the only ship from the original armada to return. She was reviewed by Her Majesty Queen Elizabeth II at the commemorations. Today she rests at Pier 35, San Francisco, fully certified by the U.S. Coast Guard and open daily to the public.
